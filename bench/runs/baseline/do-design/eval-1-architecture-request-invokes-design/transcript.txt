Summary of the design:

- **One shared queue, one envelope schema.** A `Notification API` accepts channel-agnostic
  requests (`channel: email|sms`, recipient, template_id, payload) and enqueues a single
  canonical `NotificationMessage` onto one queue. Both channels publish to and are consumed from
  the same queue — no separate email queue and SMS queue.
- **Channel routing happens in-process, not in the transport layer.** A single, horizontally
  scalable `Notification Worker Pool` consumes the shared queue. A `Channel Router` component
  reads the `channel` field on each dequeued message and dispatches to a channel `Sender`
  strategy (`EmailSender` / `SmsSender`) behind a common `NotificationSender` interface. This is
  the key structural decision: sharing the queue means sharing schema, retry/backoff policy, and
  the dead-letter queue, while channel-specific behavior (provider SDK calls, formatting,
  provider-side rate limits) stays isolated in the Sender implementations.
- **Noisy-neighbor risk is called out and mitigated.** SMS providers typically rate-limit far
  more aggressively than email providers, so a shared queue risks a slow SMS provider stalling
  email throughput. Mitigation: each Sender enforces its own provider-side backpressure
  (token-bucket) and requeues its own failures with backoff, rather than blocking the shared
  worker pool; workers are stateless and horizontally scalable. If this proves insufficient, the
  design records a fallback path — broker-native fan-out (e.g. one SNS/Kafka topic feeding two
  channel-filtered subscriber queues) — as a deferred alternative, not the initial shape.
- **API contract:** `POST /v1/notifications` (channel-agnostic request, returns
  `{notification_id, status: queued}`), `GET /v1/notifications/{id}` for delivery-status polling.
- **Data model:** a single `NotificationMessage` envelope with `channel` as the only
  discriminator field, plus `notification_id`, `recipient`, `template_id`, `payload`, `priority`,
  `idempotency_key`, `attempt_count`, `created_at`.
- Full C1 (context), C2 (container), and C3 (component) Mermaid diagrams, a sequence diagram for
  the enqueue -> route -> send -> retry/DLQ flow, and a Risks & Alternatives section (rejecting
  fully separate queues as contradicting the stated requirement, and recording broker-native
  fan-out as a considered-but-deferred alternative) are in the written design.md.
